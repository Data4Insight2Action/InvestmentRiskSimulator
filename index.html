Investment Risk Simulator

A GIC or savings account grows along a steady, predictable path. An investment in a security usually grows more over time, but it experiences ups and downs along the way, and no one knows in advance how bumpy the road will be or where it will end up.

A security’s average annual return is like a GIC’s interest rate—similar to the slope of an uphill path. The term “standard deviation” sounds technical, but it simply describes how bumpy the road is, like having speed bumps and cracks in the pavement. A GIC’s standard deviation is 0 because the path is completely flat. In fact, when a security’s standard deviation is 0, it is technically a GIC.

This simulator can run thousands of simulations to show you thousands of possible future paths for the security’s price over the next 10 years. However, if you set the number of simulations to 1, you can see a single price path that reflects how movement looks in the real world.

Do more simulations give a better prediction?

Partly. Each simulation is one possible future. With only a few, the worst, typical and best cases jump around every time you click Run. Rare bad outcomes are also easy to miss, so the worst case can look better than it really is. More simulations make these numbers steadier: 4 times as many simulations cut the jumpiness about in half.

But more simulations can’t make the forecast more accurate than the numbers you enter. If the future doesn’t match the average return and standard deviation you chose, no number of simulations will fix that.

    Ask an AI assistant for your portfolio’s metrics

    Copy the question below, fill in your holdings, and ask any AI assistant. It will give you the two numbers to enter here.

    “I hold a portfolio of [Percentage]% [Ticker/Company], [Percentage]% [Ticker/Company], and [Percentage]% [Ticker/Company]. Based on [Number] years of historical data, what are my portfolio’s average annual total return (including dividends) and standard deviation?”

    Note: You can include as many holdings as you like. Just make sure the weights add up to 100% and the tickers or company names are valid.

    Limits: This simulator accepts −150.00% to 150.00% for the average annual return and 0.00% to 150.00% for the standard deviation.

    Examples:

    Single holding:

    “I have a portfolio of 100% VFV.TO. Using 8 years of history, what are my portfolio’s average annual total return (including dividends) and standard deviation?”

    Multi-asset allocation:

    “I have a portfolio of 40% Royal Bank, 35% Enbridge, and 25% Telus. Using 10 years of history, what are my portfolio’s average annual total return (including dividends) and standard deviation?”
